# Domain Pitfalls — Dừa Yoga Landing Page

**Domain:** Yoga instructor personal brand landing page
**Researched:** 2026-06-14
**Confidence:** HIGH (cross-referenced: Unbounce conversion research, Yoga Alliance standards, Vietnamese digital behavior patterns)

---

## Critical Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Stock Photo Syndrome — Invisible Instructor

**What goes wrong:** Page uses generic yoga stock photos (flexible woman on beach, sunset poses) instead of showing the actual instructor. Visitors can't form a personal connection with a silhouette.

**Why it happens:** Easier to grab free stock photos than schedule a professional photoshoot. Or the instructor feels camera-shy.

**Consequences:** The page looks like every other yoga template. No trust, no connection, no reason to choose this instructor over 100 others. For a 1:1 private service, the instructor IS the product — hiding them is hiding the product.

**Prevention:**
- Hero section MUST feature Thảo in a real teaching environment
- Use 3-5 authentic photos: teaching, demonstrating poses, in her space
- Even phone-quality photos of the real instructor beat professional stock photos
- Budget for a 1-hour photoshoot before launch (Phase 1 dependency)

**Detection:** If someone sees the page and asks "but what does she look like?" or "where does she teach?" — you've failed this.

**Phase:** Phase 1 (Core Structure) — photoshoot must happen before or during this phase

---

### Pitfall 2: Yoga Jargon Wall — Losing Beginners

**What goes wrong:** Page uses Sanskrit terms (Vinyasa, Hatha, Ashtanga, Pranayama) without translation or context. Office workers curious about yoga see a wall of unfamiliar words and bounce.

**Why it happens:** Instructors live in yoga vocabulary daily. What's obvious to Thảo is alien to her target audience.

**Consequences:** The #1 target (office workers, beginners) feels excluded before they even understand what's offered. They think "this isn't for me" and leave.

**Prevention:**
- Always pair Sanskrit with plain Vietnamese explanation
- Use benefit-first language: "Giảm đau lưng dân văn phòng" not "Hatha Yoga cho người mới"
- Test copy with someone who has NEVER done yoga
- The "mom test": if Thảo's non-yoga friends can't understand the offer, rewrite it

**Detection:** Read each section aloud to a non-yogi. If they ask "what does that mean?" — rewrite.

**Phase:** Phase 2 (Content & Copy) — copywriting must be beginner-validated

### Pitfall 3: Missing the Zalo/Messenger Booking Bridge

**What goes wrong:** Page has a "Liên hệ" button but doesn't explain what happens next. Visitor wonders: "Do I call? Text? Wait for a reply? How long? What do I say?"

**Why it happens:** The instructor assumes booking is obvious. "Just message me" feels clear to her, vague to visitors.

**Consequences:** Hesitation kills conversions. Visitors who were ready to book abandon because the path feels uncertain. Especially critical in Vietnam where Zalo is preferred but Messenger is the fallback — unclear which to use.

**Prevention:**
- Each CTA button should have micro-copy explaining the next step
- Example: "Nhắn Zalo — Thảo phản hồi trong 30 phút"
- Show BOTH Zalo and Messenger options with clear labels
- Include a pre-written message template: "Chào Thảo, em muốn tìm hiểu về..."
- After clicking, show a confirmation: "Tin nhắn đã mở. Thảo sẽ phản hồi sớm!"

**Detection:** Click every CTA on the page. After each click, can you answer "what happens now?" in 3 seconds?

**Phase:** Phase 1 (Core Structure) — CTA flow must be designed upfront, not bolted on later

### Pitfall 4: Service Soup — Three Classes, No Clarity

**What goes wrong:** All three services (1:1, Group 1:6, Online) are listed with equal weight. Visitor can't tell which is right for them. Decision paralysis.

**Why it happens:** Instructor wants to offer everything. Fair — but the page tries to sell all three equally to everyone.

**Consequences:** Visitor thinks "I don't know which one I need" and leaves. Or picks the wrong one and has a bad first experience.

**Prevention:**
- Lead with ONE primary service (recommend: 1:1 Private for office workers)
- Use a simple decision guide: "Mới bắt đầu? → 1:1. Có nhóm bạn? → Nhóm nhỏ. Ở xa? → Online"
- Don't list all three equally — make one hero, others secondary
- Price anchoring: show 1:1 first (highest value), then group (value), then online (accessible)

**Detection:** Ask 5 people: "Which class should you take?" If they can't answer in 10 seconds, the decision framework is broken.

**Phase:** Phase 1 (Core Structure) — service hierarchy must be decided before design

### Pitfall 5: No Social Proof — "Trust Me" Isn't a Strategy

**What goes wrong:** Page has zero testimonials, no student photos, no credentials displayed. It's a beautiful page with no evidence anyone has ever taken a class.

**Why it happens:** Instructor feels awkward asking for testimonials. Or hasn't collected them yet. Or thinks "my work speaks for itself."

**Consequences:** For a personal service, social proof is everything. Without it, the visitor is asked to trust a stranger with their time and money. Most won't.

**Prevention:**
- Collect 3-5 testimonials BEFORE launch (even from friends/family who've attended)
- Include: name, photo (or initial), specific result ("Giảm đau cổ sau 2 tuần")
- Display Yoga Alliance certification prominently if available
- Show student count or years of experience as a number
- Vietnamese-specific: testimonials in Vietnamese feel more authentic than translated ones

**Detection:** Does the page answer "Why should I trust this person?" with evidence, not just claims?

**Phase:** Phase 2 (Content & Copy) — testimonial collection is a pre-launch task

## Moderate Pitfalls

### Pitfall 6: Mobile-Second Design (Backwards in Vietnam)

**What goes wrong:** Page is designed desktop-first, then squeezed to mobile. On phones — where 80%+ of Vietnamese users browse — it's cramped, slow, or broken.

**Why it happens:** Developers/designers work on desktop screens. Mobile becomes an afterthought.

**Prevention:**
- Design mobile-first. Every layout decision starts at 375px width
- Test on actual phones, not just browser dev tools
- Zalo/Messenger deep-links must work on mobile (test the actual click flow)
- Hero image must load fast on 4G — optimize to <200KB

**Detection:** Open the page on a mid-range Android phone over 4G. If it takes >3 seconds to load or requires horizontal scrolling — fix it.

**Phase:** Phase 1 (Core Structure) — mobile-first is a design constraint, not a feature

---

### Pitfall 7: Generic "About" Section — Bio Instead of Connection

**What goes wrong:** About section reads like a CV: "10 năm kinh nghiệm, chứng chỉ Yoga Alliance, chuyên về Hatha và Vinyasa." Professional but cold.

**Why it happens:** Instructors copy-paste their professional bio. It's accurate but doesn't create emotional connection.

**Prevention:**
- Lead with WHY she teaches, not WHAT she's certified in
- Include a personal moment: "Sau 8 tiếng ngồi văn phòng, tôi hiểu nỗi đau lưng của bạn"
- Keep credentials as supporting proof, not the headline
- Show personality — warm, approachable, not corporate

**Detection:** Does the About section make you want to have coffee with this person? Or does it read like a LinkedIn profile?

**Phase:** Phase 2 (Content & Copy) — copywriting task

---

### Pitfall 8: Pricing Anxiety — Hidden or Unclear

**What goes wrong:** No pricing shown. Visitor thinks "probably too expensive" and leaves. Or pricing is shown but without context — "500k/buổi" means nothing without comparison.

**Why it happens:** Fear that showing prices will scare people away. Or prices change often.

**Prevention:**
- Show at least a starting price or range: "Từ 400.000đ/buổi"
- Frame value: "Giá một buổi = 2 lần massage, nhưng hiệu quả kéo dài"
- If exact pricing varies, show the range and say "Liên hệ để nhận báo giá phù hợp"
- Never make visitors ask for pricing — that's a conversion killer

**Detection:** Would a visitor know the price range within 10 seconds of scanning the page?

**Phase:** Phase 2 (Content & Copy) — pricing strategy discussion needed

---

### Pitfall 9: No Location Context — "Where Do I Go?"

**What goes wrong:** Page mentions classes but not WHERE they happen. Is it a studio? Home? Park? District?

**Why it happens:** Instructor knows her location. Visitors don't.

**Prevention:**
- Show district/area prominently: "Quận 1, TP.HCM" or "Tại nhà Thảo, gần..."
- For online classes: mention platform (Zoom/Zalo Video)
- For group classes: mention the venue or area
- A simple map embed helps, but even just the district name is enough

**Detection:** Can a visitor know the location within 5 seconds of landing on the page?

**Phase:** Phase 1 (Core Structure) — location info is structural, not decorative

---

### Pitfall 10: Ignoring the "What If I'm Not Flexible?" Fear

**What goes wrong:** Page shows advanced poses. Beginner thinks "I can't do that" and leaves. The #1 fear of yoga beginners is flexibility judgment.

**Why it happens:** Instructors showcase their best work. But that work intimidates the very people they're trying to reach.

**Prevention:**
- Include a section: "Bạn không cần dẻo để bắt đầu yoga"
- Show modified/beginner versions of poses alongside advanced ones
- Use language like "Mọi trình độ đều được chào đón"
- Photo of a beginner doing a simple stretch > photo of instructor in full split

**Detection:** Does the page make a stiff office worker feel welcome, or intimidated?

**Phase:** Phase 2 (Content & Copy) — messaging must address beginner anxiety

## Minor Pitfalls

### Pitfall 11: Slow Image Loading

**What goes wrong:** Beautiful high-res photos that take 5+ seconds to load on mobile. Visitor bounces before seeing the page.

**Prevention:** Compress all images. Use WebP format. Lazy-load below-fold images. Hero image <200KB.

**Phase:** Phase 1 (Core Structure) — performance is a design constraint

---

### Pitfall 12: No Favicon or Meta Tags

**What goes wrong:** Browser tab shows a generic icon. Shared link on Zalo shows no preview image or description.

**Prevention:** Set favicon, Open Graph tags (og:image, og:title, og:description), and Twitter cards. Test sharing the link in Zalo before launch.

**Phase:** Phase 1 (Core Structure) — basic SEO/social setup

---

### Pitfall 13: Form Over Function — Animation Overload

**What goes wrong:** Parallax scrolling, fade-in animations, particle effects. Looks impressive in a portfolio, annoys visitors who just want to book a class.

**Prevention:** Animations should be subtle (<300ms), purposeful, and never block content. No animation on CTA buttons. Test with animations disabled — is the page still usable?

**Phase:** Phase 1 (Core Structure) — performance and UX constraints

---

### Pitfall 14: Vietnamese Text Rendering Issues

**What goes wrong:** Font doesn't support Vietnamese diacritics (ă, ơ, ư, dấu). Text shows as boxes or fallback fonts.

**Prevention:** Test with Vietnamese text containing all diacritics. Use fonts with full Vietnamese support (e.g., Inter, Be Vietnam Pro, system fonts). Verify on both iOS and Android.

**Phase:** Phase 1 (Core Structure) — font selection is foundational
